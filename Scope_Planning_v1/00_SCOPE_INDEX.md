## What this is

This is a working business plan, not a research exercise. The end goal is a small, self-sustaining business: find real operational problems inside Swedish small and medium-sized businesses, sell a service that fixes one of them, deliver it well enough that the customer pays again, and only then build out the automation and tooling that makes the whole thing repeatable at low marginal cost.

The plan is split into eight documents because trying to hold "how do we find problems," "how do we build the service," "what infrastructure do we need," "how do we know it's working," and "how do we sell it" in one document made the plan unreadable and, worse, made it easy to skip the parts that don't feel like "real work" — like actually calling a prospect before writing another line of code.

## The documents

1. **`01_Problem_and_Opportunity_Discovery.md`** — How we find evidence of real business problems and decide which ones are worth pursuing, before any solution gets designed.
2. **`02_AI_Service_Discovery_Design_and_Agent_Engineering.md`** — How a validated problem becomes an actual service offer, and only later, an automated one.
3. **`03_Technical_Architecture_and_Implementation.md`** — The technology behind the business, sequenced from what one person can run on a laptop to what a small operating company eventually needs.
4. **`04_Results_Evaluation_and_Continuous_Improvement.md`** — How we know whether any of this is actually working, for the discovery process and for delivered services alike.
5. **`05_Customer_Acquisition_Sales_Delivery_and_Autonomous_Maintenance.md`** — Finding customers, selling to them, delivering the service, and keeping them.
6. **`06_Tools_and_Cost_Reference.md`** — What everything costs, with the cheapest workable option listed first.
7. **`07_Realistic_Timeline_and_Phasing.md`** — A build order with honest time estimates, because five well-written documents can make a year of work feel like a weekend project if nobody writes down how long things actually take.
8. **`08_Service_Idea_Catalog.md`** — A wide, organized brainstorm of AI service ideas by business function and by industry, to be drawn on only after Document 1 has validated a real problem — not a shortlist or a roadmap in its own right.

## How the business is meant to move

```text
MARKET
  |
  v
0. Test the idea by hand, no code, no infrastructure
  |
  v
1. Find and document real problems businesses actually have
  |
  v
2. Decide which of those problems are worth chasing
  |
  v
3. Design a service that solves one of them
  |
  v
4. Sell it. Deliver it by hand. Get paid before automating anything.
  |
  v
5. Turn what worked into a repeatable, partly automated service
  |
  v
6. Go find more customers
  |
  v
7. Deliver at scale without the quality dropping
  |
  v
8. Keep score — is it actually working?
  |
  v
9. Feed what was learned back into step 1
  |
  +---------------------> back to the top
```

Step 4 is the one that's easiest to skip and the most expensive to skip. Selling and delivering one service by hand, before any of the "AI agent" infrastructure gets built, is what separates this plan from a stack of interesting technology with no customer behind it. Finding out a service doesn't sell after two weeks of manual outreach is a minor setback. Finding out after two months of building agents is a real loss — of time, of money, and, frankly, of motivation.

## Why this is structured as a business, not a research project

Every document downstream of this one is written to answer a business question, not a scientific one. Document 1 doesn't ask "is this an interesting pattern" — it asks "is this a problem someone will pay to have solved." Document 4 doesn't measure precision and recall for their own sake — it measures them because bad extraction quality eventually shows up as a service that doesn't work and a customer who doesn't renew. The technical work in Document 3 exists to support a paying business, and is scoped and priced accordingly in Document 6 — nothing in this plan gets built because it's technically interesting; it gets built because a paying step in the business needs it.

## The bigger picture

The discovery engine described in Document 1 is the first layer of something larger:

```text
Evidence
 -> Problems worth solving
 -> A service someone will actually pay for
 -> The automation that makes delivering it repeatable
 -> A growing base of paying customers
 -> Real business outcomes — revenue, retained customers, margin
 -> Honest measurement of whether it's working
 -> A better next round of discovery
```

Treat all eight documents as one plan. Documents 6 and 7 exist specifically to keep the rest of the plan honest about cost and time — read those two first if today's question is "can I actually do this." Document 8 exists to speed up service design once Document 1 has already found a real problem — it's a reference to consult, not a document to read start to finish.
