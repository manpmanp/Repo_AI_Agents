## What this document is, and just as important, what it isn't

This is a brainstorm, organized. It's a wide net of AI service ideas gathered by business function and by industry — a starting point for spotting where a validated problem from Document 1 might already have an obvious service shape waiting for it. It is not a shortlist, not a roadmap, and not a set of ideas that have been checked against any real evidence. Treat every entry below as a hypothesis worth testing, not a project worth starting.

That distinction matters enough to repeat: nothing in this catalog skips the process laid out in Documents 1 and 2. An idea earns the right to be built only after it's backed by real evidence of a recurring problem (Document 1), only after it's been sold and manually delivered to an actual paying customer (Document 2, Section 3), and only after that customer has said, in effect, "yes, this was worth paying for." A clever-sounding idea from this list with no evidence behind it is exactly the trap the rest of this plan is built to avoid.

## How to actually use this list

Start from a validated problem, not from this catalog. When Document 1's discovery process turns up a real, evidenced pattern — say, small businesses in a given trade struggling with manual scheduling — come back here to see whether a recognizable service shape already exists for it (a Booking Assistant, in that example) or whether the real answer is something narrower and more specific to what the evidence actually showed. The catalog exists to speed up the "what would this look like as a service" step, not to replace the validation step that has to come before it.

## Service ideas by business function

**Sales & Lead Generation**

- Follow-up Assistant — automatic reminders, follow-up emails and texts, recovering leads that went cold, simple re-engagement campaigns

**Customer Service**

**Administration**

**Human Resources**

**Marketing**

**Finance**

**Legal & Compliance**

**Operations**

## Bundled, industry-specific assistants

Rather than selling a generic function (scheduling, invoicing, customer support) on its own, a service can be packaged as a single assistant built around one specific trade — closer to what an actual small business owner would recognize and want to buy. Examples worth testing against real evidence as they come up: a hair salon assistant, a barber shop assistant, a dental clinic assistant, a veterinary clinic assistant, a physiotherapy clinic assistant, a construction office assistant, an electrician's office assistant, a plumbing office assistant, an auto repair shop assistant, a cleaning company assistant, a restaurant manager assistant, a café assistant, an accounting office assistant, a law office assistant, a real estate agency assistant, a retail store assistant, an e-commerce assistant, a manufacturing assistant, a logistics assistant, and a hotel assistant.

This framing lines up directly with what Document 1's seed evidence already found — a plumbing business with a strong website but no capacity to take on more work needs a different assistant than a hairdresser with no booking system at all, even though both might get called "an AI office assistant" in a pitch deck. The bundled framing is a packaging decision for later, once a function-level service has already proven itself — not a reason to skip straight to building an all-in-one assistant before any single piece of it has been sold.

## Multi-agent business systems — a later-stage model, not a starting point

A further-out version of this business sells a coordinated system of specialized agents rather than one standalone tool — a Sales Agent, Marketing Agent, Customer Support Agent, HR Agent, Recruitment Agent, Finance Agent, Bookkeeping Agent, Scheduling Agent, Operations Agent, Compliance Agent, Knowledge Agent, Analytics Agent, Executive Agent, and Decision Support Agent, all working together as something closer to a complete AI workforce than a single tool.

This is real, and worth keeping in view — but it belongs firmly at Phase 6 and beyond in Document 7's timeline. It depends on several individual services already having been sold, delivered, and proven, and on the skill and orchestration foundations from Document 2 already being solid. Reaching for this model before a single service has a paying customer behind it is exactly the over-building this plan is structured to avoid.

## How this catalog actually gets narrowed down

This list is deliberately broad and deliberately unranked — narrowing it down is Document 1's job, not this document's. The Opportunity Scoring process defined there (Document 1, Section on scoring) is what actually decides which of these ideas, if any, are worth pursuing next: how strong the evidence is, how much economic value is at stake, how big the gap in existing solutions really is, how well AI genuinely suits the problem, and how commercially realistic it looks. An idea from this catalog only becomes a real candidate once it's been run through that process and come out the other side backed by real evidence — not because it sounded promising on a first read.
